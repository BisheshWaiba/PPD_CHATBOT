Why we built Sutkeri

The weeks after a baby arrives are full of questions, and a mother's own health is often the last thing anyone asks about.

Postpartum depression is common, and many mothers never talk about it. A short screening can help her see how she is really doing and decide whether to speak with a doctor. But it has to be easy to reach, open at any hour, and available in a language she is comfortable in.

Sutkeri is that first step. It asks gentle questions one at a time, explains what the answers mean in plain words, and points to real help when it is needed. It does not diagnose, and it does not replace a doctor, a midwife or a health worker.

Our mission

To empower every mother with the knowledge, support, and confidence she needs to thrive during her postpartum journey, creating a world where no mother feels alone or unprepared.

What you can do with Sutkeri

Everything starts with a conversation. Sign in, and the assistant takes it from there.

  • Talk it through

    Ask what postpartum depression is, what causes it and what the signs look like, or ask for practical self-care tips. Sutkeri answers one step at a time.

  • Take a short screening

    Answer the 10 questions of the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS) in English or Nepali, and get a score with plain-language guidance.

  • Bring a report to your doctor

    Your results page shows your score, your answers and what to do next, and you can print it for your appointment.

  • Find help right away

    Nepal's crisis and mental health helplines are listed in the chat and on your results page, including 1166, which is free from Nepal Telecom.

How we keep it safe and honest

A mother using Sutkeri may be tired, worried or low. These are the rules we hold the product to.

A screening, not a diagnosis

The EPDS is a widely used questionnaire that helps start a conversation with a health professional. Sutkeri never gives a diagnosis, and what it says is not a substitute for medical care.

Help is never hidden

If an answer suggests a mother may be thinking of harming herself, Sutkeri says so plainly and points to crisis helplines straight away. Crisis numbers are also on the home page.

Your information, plainly

Your screening answers are saved only in your own browser and cleared when you log out. Your password is never stored in readable form.

Gentle by design

One question at a time, in plain words. You can stop whenever you need to.

Goals we work toward

Three of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals guide what we build. Here is what each one means for a mother using Sutkeri.

Goal 3

A first step toward care

Postpartum depression is common and often goes unspoken. Sutkeri offers the 10-question EPDS screening, explains the score in plain language, and lists Nepal helplines, so a mother knows when to reach out for care.

Goal 5

Her wellbeing comes first

New mothers often carry a baby's care alone while their own health waits. Sutkeri asks how she is doing, not only how the baby is, and it is built for partners and family to use alongside her.

Goal 10

Support that is easy to reach

Where you live and the language you speak should not decide whether you get support. Sutkeri works in a web browser on a phone or a computer, offers the screening in Nepali and English, and points to Nepal-specific helplines.
