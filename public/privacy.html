3. Why we use it

  • To run your account and let you sign in.
  • To show you your chats and your results, and to let you print them for your health provider.
  • To write the replies in the chat (see the next section).
  • To email you about your account, only when you ask for something or sign up.
  • To keep the service safe: to slow down password guessing and to find and fix faults.

We do not sell your information, and we do not use it for advertising. You agree to this policy when you create your account, and you can withdraw by deleting your account at any time.

4. The AI conversation

When the AI conversation is switched on, an AI model writes Sutkeri's replies when you just talk (how you feel, your baby, everyday talk). It does not write the guided questions, the check-in or its results, the helplines, or the reply to a message about harming yourself or your baby: those are written in advance.

To write a reply, the most recent messages of that chat, up to 16, are sent to Anthropic, PBC, the company that runs the AI model. These can include what you typed in the guided questions, such as your age and your baby's birth date, and Sutkeri's earlier replies. We do not send your name, your email address, or anything that identifies your account. We cannot stop you from typing your name, a phone number or an address into the chat, so please do not.

Anthropic handles that text under its own terms and privacy policy. [[ADD A LINK TO ANTHROPIC'S CURRENT PRIVACY POLICY AND API TERMS, AND STATE IN A SENTENCE WHAT THOSE TERMS SAY ABOUT HOW LONG IT KEEPS API DATA AND WHETHER IT TRAINS ON IT. CHECK THIS FOR YOURSELF.]]

The AI can be wrong. It is told never to diagnose, never to give advice about medicines, and to send you to a health provider or the hospital for physical warning signs. It is not a substitute for any of them. When the AI is switched off, nothing is sent to anyone, and the chat uses written replies instead.

8. How we keep it safe

Pages are sent over HTTPS. Passwords are stored only in scrambled form. A sign-in stops working when you log out, when it runs out, and when your password is changed. Repeated wrong passwords are slowed down. Access to the database is limited to the people who run Sutkeri. No system is perfectly safe. If something happens that puts your information at risk, we will tell you [[HOW AND HOW QUICKLY YOU WILL TELL PEOPLE]].
